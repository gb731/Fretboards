Triads, CAGED shapes, and chord progressions in every key and mode

Pick a key and a mode, then choose which chords to show. Each chord has its own color, and where two chords share a note the dot is split. Every fretboard below follows your selection.

On a phone each fretboard is wider than the screen. Swipe it sideways to see the whole neck, or turn Zoom on to focus on a few frets.

Progression order for the individual fretboards

Only chords switched on above appear here. Tap them in the order you want, such as 1, 3, 4, 6. The individual fretboards then show those chords in that order, repeats included. Tap a step to remove it, or Clear to return to the normal order.

CAGED shapes on the individual chord fretboards

Dashed bands mark where each open-chord shape lands for that chord. Tap any combination of letters, such as C, A and D, to see the forms above and below each other. Notes outside the chosen shapes fade. Minor chords use the minor form (labeled like "Am shape") in the same positions. Of the minor forms, Am, Em and Dm are the ones guitarists really play. The Cm and Gm shapes are awkward and rarely used, but they are shown so the five-shape pattern stays complete. Diminished chords are left out.

Pad plays a soft synth chord under each step, voiced to move as little as possible from chord to chord. Loop repeats the progression until you press Stop. While it loops you can change the tempo, drums, mix, or chord order, and the change takes effect on the next pass.

Tap any note to hear it, or tap a shape outline to hear it strummed from the low string up. Each individual chord's Play button strums one of its outlined shapes, nearest the anchor fret, so it follows your string set, zoom, and CAGED choices. If outlines are off, it picks a shape on any strings. Play progression plays your numbered steps in order, or all switched-on chords if no progression is set, with the drums keeping time at the same tempo. On iPad, make sure the silent switch is off.

Solid dots are the current chord. Dashed rings show the previous chord's shape, and notes with a dark ring are shared by both chords.
